I stand by both rows. Here is the argument for each, against the rules as written.

Point a: Memphis scheduled should be 4.00 because shifts 5 and 6 cross the window edge.

Rule 3 says scheduled hours are the union of active shift intervals clipped to the reporting window. "Clipped" means the part outside the window is cut off and the part inside is kept. If the rule intended straddling shifts to be excluded, it would say "contained in" or "fully within" the window, not "clipped to". The word only has meaning if partial shifts survive.

Rule 6 defines the window as a half-open range, [10-12 00:00, 10-19 00:00). It describes a time range to measure, not a membership test on shift records. Shift 5 has 4 real hours inside that range, from 00:00 to 04:00 on 10-12. Shift 6 has 4 real hours inside it, from 20:00 on 10-18 to 00:00 on 10-19. Those hours were scheduled, they fall inside the week, and someone either worked them or they went dark.

The operational test settles it. Under the colleague's reading, every overnight shift that crosses Sunday midnight disappears from both the week it starts in and the week it ends in, so it is never reported anywhere. That is exactly the kind of gap the scheduling screen would show and the KPI would miss, which is the production bug you are chasing. Clipping also makes weeks sum correctly: the 2 hours of shift 5 on 10-11 belong to the prior week, the 4 hours on 10-12 to this one, and nothing is lost or counted twice.

Memphis scheduled stays at 12.00. Rows for 5 and 6 stay as I gave them: 240 minutes each after clipping.

Point b: Coverage 107 should count 2.00 hours because somebody was on site.

Rule 4 says covered hours are the union of coverage intervals clipped to active scheduled intervals and the reporting window. Coverage is only meaningful as coverage of something scheduled. From 07:00 to 08:00 on 10-16 there is no active Memphis shift, so there is nothing for that hour to cover.

Rule 5 shows why the rule is written that way. Dark is scheduled minus covered and must never go negative. If coverage outside any shift counted, a short shift with a long coverer would produce covered greater than scheduled, and the only way to keep dark non-negative would be an arbitrary floor that hides real gaps elsewhere. Clipping coverage to the scheduled intervals is what makes the subtraction honest.

The colleague's point that the person was on site is true, and the record does exist. That is an audit or payroll fact, and the record should stay in the table untouched. The KPI question is different. The KPI asks how many scheduled hours were covered, and from 07:00 to 08:00 the answer is zero because none were scheduled. The report and the screen can both show that extra hour as unscheduled presence if the business wants it, but it cannot reduce dark hours.

Coverage 107 stays at 60 minutes, clipped to 10-16 08:00 to 09:00. Memphis covered stays at 6.00 and dark at 6.00.
